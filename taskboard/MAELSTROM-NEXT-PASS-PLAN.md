# Maelstrom visual and encounter improvement plan

Prepared by Codex, 13 November 2026, for the overnight handoff and Saturday work. This is a proposed implementation plan, not completed work. It extends [the timed encounter design](MAELSTROM-TIMED-BOSS-DESIGN.md). Claude currently owns the running editor and overnight queue; read its latest STATUS and changes before claiming implementation. No gameplay or asset files were changed to prepare this plan.

The target is a terrifying journey into an enormous collapsing sea: a dark tunnel opens onto luminous sky, the bow reaches a lip, the floor falls away, and a broad spiral current carries the ship around an unseen leviathan. The crater remains the largest presence in the frame. Water geometry, foam, spray, lighting and steering must describe the same moving surface.

## What must change in the picture

The baseline still shows a nearly complete enclosing rim, repeated large blue folds, bright contour-like foam, isolated white patches and soft detached mist balls. Together these read as a modelled basin rather than a vast turbulent sea. The horizon and foreground also reveal too much of the structure at once.

Make five changes before cosmetic detail:

1. Compose a view down across the slope with a visible middle distance and central depth. Keep one dark tunnel edge briefly in the foreground and move the reveal sightline clear of the mast.
2. Break the perimeter into a few strong, unequal sectors: a broad low overflow, a distant high cascade, a dark rock interruption, a partially concealed ruined headland. Avoid regularly spaced buttresses and a uniform waterfall crown.
3. Give water three readable scales: large wave groups that change silhouette, mid-distance breakers with connected foam, and restrained close ripples. The current repeated folds must not become the dominant scale cue.
4. Separate depth with selective spray and atmospheric perspective. Preserve clear windows into the drop; fog should obscure selected forms rather than fill the whole crater.
5. Make the main light cross the scene. Sunlit spray and crest edges against shaded water should establish depth before bloom or extra light shafts are added.

## A spiral current channel that explains steering limits

Build a broad, shallow riding channel into the descending water, following an authored spiral centreline. Its outer shoulder is an uphill counter-current and breaking wave bank. Its inner shoulder is a lower, fast-moving crest separating the navigable flow from the plunging water. Foam, spray and crest travel make both boundaries legible.

This should look like a standing hydraulic wave within a powerful current, not two continuous solid walls or a perfectly circular racetrack. Break the shoulders into overlapping wave groups with uneven heights and crest gaps. The underlying return current remains continuous through the gaps. Keep the inner shoulder low enough to see the abyss and aim at targets beyond it. Fog can partially cover the outer shoulder but must not hide both edges simultaneously.

Initial tuning values, to be tested against the actual ship dimensions:

| Property | Starting range | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navigable width | 120–200 m total, or roughly 8–12 ship beams | Enough room for meaningful tornado dodges without losing a readable course. |
| Outer shoulder rise | 8–20 m over a broad flank | Visible uphill resistance and backlit breaking crest. |
| Inner shoulder rise | 3–8 m above local channel | Visible boundary without hiding the crater and gun targets. |
| Large crest groups | 60–180 m along the route, with unequal gaps | Avoid a tiled wall while keeping hazards readable ahead. |
| Encounter travel speed | Trial 8–12 m/s after descent | Slow enough for aiming, with local foam and spray conveying more violence than travel speed alone. |

A complete revolution is not required. At a 1 km radius, even 10 m/s takes about ten minutes to circle once. For a shorter musical encounter, use a credible partial orbit; do not spin a kilometre-scale environment rapidly just to complete a lap.

Store progress `s` along the route, lateral offset `d`, lateral velocity, and a local tangent/across/up frame. Integrate steering with inertia. In the outer portion of the corridor, increase restoring force smoothly as the ship climbs a shoulder. Feed climbing into roll, modest speed loss, spray and a short creak/crew cue. Release of the helm should visibly let the current carry the ship back. Avoid a hard invisible clamp as the normal steering response; retain only an emergency numerical limit beyond the visible shoulder.

Represent this in the existing ship-fixed/world-moving architecture. Derive the arena transform from the chosen path position and tangent instead of accumulating independent world offsets. This is a maelstrom route adapter, not a rewrite of the entire treadmill.

Use one surface/path description for the rendered banks, ship ride height and lean, hazard positions, cannon impact sampling, boss water contact and VFX emitters. Large gameplay waves must agree between CPU queries and visible geometry. Small cosmetic ripples can stay shader-only. Check steep slopes and the route seam explicitly.

Steering acceptance: held full left and full right visibly climb the appropriate bank, slow and settle; neither crosses a hidden plane, exposes a hole, intersects the hull, nor removes every safe hazard lane. At each battery, target windows remain in range and above the intervening crest.

## Tunnel exit and rollercoaster emergence

Suggested timings are relative to exit and are tuning values. Keep them editable and separate from the combat duration.

| Beat | Starting duration | Visual and motion direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final tunnel approach | Last 5–8 seconds inside | Dark close curling water, overhead cloud volume, bright opening expanding ahead, racing peripheral spray. No early view through to the entire arena. |
| Threshold | About 1–2 seconds | Brief glare and luminous spray; rigging stays readable. The dark tunnel edge passes behind the player naturally. |
| Crest anticipation | About 2–4 seconds | Motion eases. Show the far ruin rim and sky first while the near crest conceals the depth. Let the distant roar arrive. |
| Drop reveal | About 4–7 seconds | The bow crosses the crest; near occlusion falls away to expose successive slope layers and the abyss. Pitch and speed grow progressively. |
| Sweeping descent | About 8–15 seconds | Join the spiral, accelerate past nearby foam, then settle into the slower combat current. Give the player time to look and understand steering before an attack. |

Use a clear helm view offset around the mast, with restrained framing assistance and retained look control. Keep the gun cameras stable. Judge the sequence at normal speed: a static bright screenshot does not prove that the transition works. Do not substitute a white flash for the geometry continuity.

For the final escape, let the defeated creature and current visibly collapse, then pass through motivated spray/occlusion into open sea. Restore ocean, exposure, weather, cameras and controls once. A clean cut is acceptable if motion and sound carry it; an unexplained teleport is not.

## Water geometry and shader work

### Shape before texture

Retain the custom basin and build the spiral riding surface into its height function. Refine the mesh near the ship and route; keep distant geometry cheaper. Add broad travelling wave groups with varied directions and envelopes, plus a few asymmetrical crest meshes where a real overhang or breaking silhouette is required. Height displacement alone cannot form a curling overhang.

Avoid solving the whole 3.2 km bowl with a new real-time fluid simulation. Authored current, analytical surface waves and local effects fit the time available and give reproducible gameplay.

Replace the broad hull shelf with a locally calmer riding patch that joins the route smoothly. Make the ship follow the low-frequency surface using fore/aft and port/starboard samples, filtered for stable deck movement and aiming. Until that is working, reduce the existing flattening footprint cautiously rather than removing it and leaving water cutting through the deck.

### A common flow field

Express water flow in basin-local surface coordinates so it survives arena rotation. Combine spiral current, inward drainage, local crest motion and temporary attack disturbances. Foam, normals, wake and spray direction should agree. The existing two-phase flow blending is a useful base, but does not by itself create evolving foam or genuine breaking geometry.

Keep the DRW normal/foam bridge optional at runtime and test it against the procedural fallback. Transform sampled detail into the curved surface frame; do not shade the displaced slope as flat ocean. Recompute or derive normals consistently for the base bowl, channel shoulders and animated displacement. Give steep falls their own distance-scaled mapping so a flat XY projection does not stretch into bands.

### Foam with causes and lifetime

Produce whitewater at actual steep/compressing crests, waterfall contact, the ship wake and boss impacts. Break it into fresh dense foam, older stretched streaks and sparse dissipating bubbles. Keep dark water between patches; uniform whitening erases depth.

First implement source masks tied to the same crest/overflow geometry, directionally advected patterns and varied decay. If these still look painted on, add a small route-local foam history render target, with source, advection and decay. Do not begin with an expensive high-resolution simulation for the whole arena. Reset/reproject history safely on seek and route transitions.

Use lower specularity and higher roughness for dense foam, richer blue-green absorption in deep water, and controlled crest scattering. Preserve moving reflections on open water. Keep micro-normal detail below the point where it shimmers or turns into metallic glitter at distance. Review foam highlights and weak-point highlights with DLSS on and off to catch trails and disappearing fine features.

## VDBs and spray placement

Use the installed tunnel for the overhead cloud throat and exit framing first. Evaluate all ten variants from the actual route and select one or two. Retint away from the demo's brown/teal look, keep emission low, and light them consistently with the scene.

A VDB is baked volume data, not automatically a collision surface or a new water simulation. Animated SVTs can supply moving airborne shapes; their use does not replace water displacement, reflections or ship interaction. Epic describes this workflow in [Sparse Volume Textures](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sparse-volume-textures-in-unreal-engine) and [Heterogeneous Volumes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/heterogeneous-volumes-in-unreal-engine). The online pages currently default to 5.9; verify controls and rendering in the installed 5.8 build.

Use three effect distances:

- **Near the ship:** short-lived Niagara droplets, sheets and impact splashes, with EasyFog wisps where they survive the gun/deck viewpoints. Emit at hull contact, breaking crests and collisions rather than everywhere.
- **Middle distance:** a few detailed, lit volume plumes at major waterfall feet and boss disturbances. Trial masked/reoriented parts of the cloud tunnel only where their motion plausibly resembles spray. Reject obvious smoke rings and slow cauliflower shapes; a cloud loop is not guaranteed to work as whitewater.
- **Far rim:** cheaper broken mist bands and sparse silhouettes. Reserve detail for the reveal sightline rather than duplicating the entire VDB library around the circumference.

Prototype one plume before scattering it. Test intersections with water, clouds, transparent effects and the tunnel; existing overnight reports mention cloud systems masking each other. Material clipping of a volume does not necessarily remove its bounds or rendering cost. Avoid a stack of overlapping giant volumes and never depend on a volume's default view distance covering the entire crater.

Use different phase offsets and modest speed variation to hide loops. Arbitrary bending of a cached tunnel around the basin is a separate material/coordinate experiment, not a promised one-click adaptation. Preload the selected sequence before the reveal; check startup streaming, loop seams, camera turns and packaged cooking. Keep only referenced variants in the build where possible.

No extra pack is required before this trial. A whitewater-specific loop is the next candidate only if cloud motion fails at waterfall contact; a tornado volume is useful only after the hazard's route, tell and damage are working.

## Lighting and depth

Start with the actual sky sun and large cloud shadow shapes. Put a luminous opening behind and to one side of the focal view; create cool shade under the nearer cloud mass and warm edges on spray, ship timber and the far ruin silhouette. Leave the deck readable without flattening it with excessive fill.

Use restrained near haze, separated middle-distance spray banks and a lighter, lower-contrast far rim. Leave a diagonal viewing window down the slope. Clouds should have unequal masses and a clear opening, not a repeated row of cotton banks.

Prefer shafts explained by a visible cloud opening and aligned with the sun. Audit the three existing spot shafts: keep them only where their directions and falloff support that same source. Raise shaft contrast by arranging shadow and scattering density, rather than increasing fog everywhere or creating bright cones floating above the water. Validate shadowing between the chosen volume system and sky in 5.8 before assuming it works.

Tune exposure through the full dark-to-light passage. The initial glare may feel overwhelming, but must resolve before the player needs to steer or read a weak point. Capture fixed-exposure diagnostics as well as the intended exposure response to distinguish better lighting from simply making the frame brighter.

## Boss presence and combat choreography

Keep the full Claw body submerged. The first reveal belongs to the crater. Establish the creature through a distant travelling bulge, a huge shadow where the water material supports it, disappearing sections of foam, one partial ridge or tentacle, and a slow low-frequency displacement reaching the ship. Avoid displaying a complete familiar-sized animal beside the hull.

Use the Claw asset for selected silhouette/animation moments and visible attack anatomy only after checking its available animations, bounds and skin. Do not merely attach unrelated tentacles to visible claws and expect a cohesive creature. New tentacles need tapered form, plausible joints, detailed surface flow, wet highlights and continuous water contact.

Each attack should have four visible beats: anticipation, committed motion, contact, recovery. Anchor effect emission to the moving anatomy and sampled waterline. Add displaced water, draining sheets, spray and a small delayed wake. These are what connect the creature to the ocean and make its movement feel heavy.

| Mechanic | Visual language | Gameplay proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tornado ahead | Disturbed spiral on water, rising spray column, clear winding-up sound, then moving funnel | At least one reachable safe route; visible damage to ropes/sails on contact. |
| Boulder/fireball | Readable boss release, trackable projectile, brief predicted impact cue | Real deck impact, local fire and crew response; no hidden instant damage. |
| Adds | Distinct approaching wake and silhouette, separate from foam noise | Shot interception or an actual boarding consequence. |
| Weak spot | Armour parts or anatomy turns to expose a glowing wound; highlight follows its surface | Reachable from a working gun; strong hit reaction, capped scoring and clear closure. |
| Finale | Tentacles lose strength, joints extinguish, bulk withdraws, water reacts and path opens | Happens on the encounter clock with attacks stopped and damage scoring closed. |

Avoid permanent bright spheres as the final weak-point presentation. Give the wound a readable shape and value contrast; use a restrained HUD marker only when visibility requires it. Place targets to suit broadside firing, not just the most dramatic central pose. The present near-arm range checks do not prove a distant central boss is hittable: measure cannon reach and elevation on every route segment.

Start the combat clock after the reveal and descent settle. Keep the existing requested phase structure: teach one dodge and exposure, develop tornado lanes, introduce fire and one add type, then a controlled final combination. Stagger helm demands and cannon openings so a solo player can switch stations or delegate. Phase changes must alter anatomy and attack motion as well as numbers.

Remove the six-break early ending. At configurable music time T the boss dies; player damage controls score and visible injury/staggers within that schedule. Track cumulative ship damage separately from repairable hull. Test no shooting, excellent shooting and damaged sails; all surviving runs must reach the same scheduled finale without an attack or projectile leaking into open sea.

## Saturday order and acceptance gates

1. **Baseline and route:** capture the current normal-speed passage; verify source/binary match. Implement the spiral path and shared surface sampling with inexpensive diagnostic materials. Prove steering, slope contact, camera and gun reach before adding visual cost.
2. **One convincing reveal:** tune lip occlusion, descent, irregular rim, cloud opening and exposure. Produce a 20–30 second normal-speed clip from inside the tunnel into the crater. The drop must read without HUD narration.
3. **One finished stretch of water:** bring the near channel, one breaker and one waterfall contact to target. Match displacement, normals, foam and spray; then extend around visible route sectors. Compare directly with the reference from the gameplay camera.
4. **One complete combat loop:** route dodge, weak-point exposure, satisfying cannon hit, timed defeat, sea return and score. Then add fire, boarding and phase variants without breaking the loop.
5. **Atmosphere and boss detail:** add the selected VDBs, contact effects, cloth/rigging consequences and lighting refinement. Remove redundant fog and lights as new layers land.
6. **Packaged build on both machines:** check missing content, shader compilation, volume streaming, inputs, music, quality levels and full voyage. Keep the most recent known-good package throughout.

Suggested quality target: stable 60 fps without frame generation at the chosen desktop output/upscaling settings, then profile the laptop and set its own quality tier. Measure frame time, GPU cost and memory in the actual packaged scene, not an empty volume trial or a concurrently busy editor. At 60 fps the full frame budget is 16.7 ms; budget effects from the measured baseline. Reduce volume overlap/resolution and offscreen VFX before reducing route readability or weak-point visibility.

Required review views: tunnel approach, threshold, lip, downward reveal, both channel shoulders at full steering, both cannon stations during exposure, tornado dodge/contact, fire/add event, and finale/sea return. Include motion clips for water flow, volumes, attacks and exposure; stills alone miss popping, repeating loops and temporal ghosting.

Before declaring a pass done, reject these visible failures: a constant waterfall ring, repeated ribs, stretched stone or water, floating fog balls, straight volume bounds, a polished hull shelf, disconnected spray, invisible route limits, a close exposed boss that miniaturises the crater, missed cannon reach, or a white flash concealing broken continuity.

Sunday starts with a tested deliverable, not a fresh rendering architecture. If time tightens, preserve the readable reveal, steerable route, one complete scored timed fight and reliable sea return; cut secondary attack variants and redundant effects first.
