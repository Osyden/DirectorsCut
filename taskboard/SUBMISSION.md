## Made during the jam

All game code (C++ module `DirectorsCut`), the ride and its phases, the sea/tunnel/whirlpool/lava material, the procedural kraken (arms and body), rocks, ruins, statues and ghost-ship treatment, rain and embers, the synthesised weather and thunder audio, the voice-order pipeline, crew behaviour, HUD. Materials are generated by the Python scripts in `Tools/`.

## How AI was used (for the description)

- **In the game:** the crew understands spoken or typed orders (local Whisper for speech-to-text, a keyword grammar for intent), answers and reports hazards in their own words (local 4B language model), and speaks (local Piper voices). Everything runs on the player's machine; no network.
- **In development:** code, materials and tuning were written by AI coding agents (Claude Code, with Codex for repository setup) directed by the owner, using the Unreal MCP server and headless editor scripts.

## Draft answers for the NVIDIA questions

- **Feature used:** NVIDIA DLSS 4.5 for Unreal Engine (plugin v8.7.2, NGX 310.6): DLSS Super Resolution at Quality (67%) is on by default. DLSS Frame Generation is integrated and supported but off by default.
- **Why:** the ride leans on hardware ray-traced Lumen, volumetric fog and clouds, a displaced ocean and many dynamic lights (lightning, deck fires, lanterns). Rendering at 67% and upscaling with DLSS keeps that affordable.
- **Not used:** the NvRTX engine branch (RTXDI, ReSTIR) and NVIDIA ACE.
- To verify in a build: the log prints `NVIDIA NGX DLSS supported DLSS-SR=1` and `DLSS requested: super resolution at 67%`.

## Build size

Done on 12 Nov: the AI servers were switched from CUDA builds to a Vulkan build of llama.cpp and a CPU build of whisper.cpp with the smaller base.en speech model. Measured on this PC: replies are as fast as before (about 240 tokens a second) and a spoken order registers in about 0.4 to 0.7 seconds. This removed about 2.7 GB and no longer needs an NVIDIA card for the crew AI.

The packaged build is now about 5 GB: roughly 1.5 GB of game, 3.1 GB language model, and 0.4 GB of speech model, voices and servers.

If there is a size limit (itch.io's normal limit is about 1 GB per file), the remaining cuts are:

1. Swap the language model for a 1 to 2 GB one. Crew lines get a little plainer.
2. Ship without the language model: crew fall back to their written lines. Saves 3.1 GB.
3. Host the build elsewhere and link it from the itch.io page, if the rules allow.
