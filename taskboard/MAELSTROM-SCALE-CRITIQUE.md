# Maelstrom scale review — 13 November

The owner compared the implementation with the ship-at-the-lip reference and rejected the exposed boss: it makes the environment look small. The correction is to make the crater itself carry the reveal. The boss must remain under the water.

## What the previous pass got wrong

The encounter worked, but those functional checks did not establish the visual result. A stated diameter of 3.2 km was not sufficient evidence of scale.

- The exposed creature occupied most of the open view. It concealed the basin and made the surrounding ruins and waves look like props beside a toy-sized pool.
- The automatic camera moved ahead of the wheel and outside the rail. That lost the wheel/crew/deck as familiar foreground size references.
- The radial profile eased flat at its outer edge. The reference has an unmistakable high overflow face, a broad descent and a low inner basin; our smooth dish lacked those separate forms.
- The replacement initially retained a rounded shoulder on the approach. That shoulder blocked the sightline into the bottom about 77 m ahead of the ship. Camera height alone could not repair it. The entry now starts at a downhill grade while the far overflow keeps its softer lip.
- Repeated isolated ruin islands broke up the circumference. Their large, similar silhouettes made it difficult to read one distant continuous rim.
- Water detail had a uniform scale and contrast. Fine glitter and narrow repeated foam lines did not describe massive folds, falling sheets, broken crests or spray at several distances.
- The first attempted waterfall replacement repeated evenly spaced bright strands. A strong rim silhouette still needs irregular water motion and a broken base to look natural.

## Implemented correction

Keep the 3.2 km diameter and deepen the basin to 900 m. A roughly 240 m overflow face runs into the inner slope. The approach side has a broader ramp, preserving one continuous height field for water, projectile queries and the encounter. The ship remains near the upper lip for the initial reveal and finishes its descent above the bottom, maintaining a view into deep water.

The camera stays behind the wheel and moves toward the starboard side, raised to see beyond the rail, with the crater centre to starboard of the bow. An aft, downward-looking camera was tested and rejected because it filled the frame with deck. A connected rim supports smaller ruins and occasional statues. These are distance markers rather than isolated hero islands.

The Kaiju body and its shadow remain hidden. The first 22 seconds after the breach are free of tentacles; staggered peeks then suggest a large submerged threat. Attacks begin at 65 seconds. Slimmer tentacles rise from local submerged roots; their joints remain reachable by the cannons. Six breaks still win the encounter and trigger the sea exit after a 20-second retreat.

Water uses broader irregular folds, broken crest foam and less fine glitter. A dedicated lit cascade follows the actual rim surface, with downward-moving branching streams and a frayed masked base. Mist sits at the falls instead of filling the entire view.

## Visual acceptance

Judge an empty reveal before adding threats. The wheel and crew must anchor the foreground; the far rim must form a readable high face across the view; the inner slope must lead visibly downward; the central abyss must stay unobstructed. There must be a perceptible difference between nearby water, the middle distance and the far architecture.

Inspect normal-speed passage as well as still frames. The lip must not pop into a straight wall as the tube disappears, the new materials must not shimmer into stripes, and tentacles must not recreate the previous obstruction. Rendering and gameplay validation do not replace an honest comparison with the reference.

## Remaining gap to the reference

The structure now exposes a deep bowl and continuous far rim without the full creature occupying the view. This is a correction to scale and staging, not cinematic sign-off. The current camera uses the ship and crew as foreground anchors but does not reproduce the central wheel and hands; rigging still crosses the vista.

The next art pass should break the waterfall silhouette into unequal overflow sections and ledges, add spray where falling water meets the slope, and vary the rim with groups of temples and broken aqueducts rather than evenly spaced landmarks. Cloud openings and light shafts need deliberate composition against this view. Those changes should be judged together in one hero shot, not accepted as separate feature checkboxes.

Water still needs comparison in motion: foam must read as textured flowing whitewater rather than painted islands, and the hull shelf must join the surrounding chop convincingly. Tentacle skin and water contact are also below the reference's finish. Keep the full body concealed throughout that work.
