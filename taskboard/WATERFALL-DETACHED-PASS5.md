Contact means the geometric mean receiving surface; live shader waves can still move above and below it.

## Verification and limits

`Tools/probe_detached_waterfalls.py` compiles the production trajectory header and actual rendered-cliff/contact functions with the existing CPU math shim. On the starting pass-4 geometry it accepted two source-8 arcs: 3.43 m and 9.54 m peak separations, lasting 1.058 s and 0.987 s. All nine default receiving-water contacts remain supported; source-8 first contact has zero measured drift. Across nine tracks per arc, the production samples satisfy the constant-horizontal/downward-gravity equation within 0.01 cm vertical and 0.05 cm horizontal tolerances. No GPU or Unreal instance was used.

The existing contact probe now explicitly extracts the attached sampler for its default/off-state regression; the new probe covers optional arcs. `DirectorsCut.Maelstrom.DetachedWaterfallTrajectory` adds engine checks for endpoint continuity, gravity, obstacle rejection, covered-water rejection and unchanged first contact. Root must build/run those tests, rerun the CPU probe after merging the new cliff candidate, and inspect a close paired capture with/without `DCDetachedFalls`.

This is not visual acceptance. Remaining concerns include visibility of a few-metre air gap at the deck camera, constant material advection speed versus physical flight acceleration, whether the existing translucent material gives enough sheet breakup, and whether the compact splash sheets read as water rather than ribbons. No new volume cache should be baked until the path and geology are accepted. The terrain can invalidate an arc; the helper rejects it instead of forcing penetration.
