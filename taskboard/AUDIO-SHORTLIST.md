## Storm bell requested by the owner

A ship or church bell should slowly begin ringing by itself as the storm builds. This is the owner's requested sound direction. Both church-bell candidates below are extracted locally under `E:\Projects\DirectorsCut\Downloads\Sonniss-GDC2026\Bell-candidates\Ivo Vicic - Church Bells`.

- Primary audition candidate, `04 Church Bells, Near Distance, In Church Tower-3 Different Bell 02.wav` (part 3, 70.15 s, stereo, 96 kHz/24-bit). Find a clean isolated strike and preserve its decay. Three different bells are described in the filename, so choose one consistent bell for the ship. A church tower recording may retain room acoustics that need evaluating.
- Alternative, `25 Church Bells, Far Distance, Rural Soundscape Distant, Hills Ridge, Spring 02.wav` (part 3, 90.58 s, stereo, 48 kHz/24-bit). Audition for a bell across the water or from a submerged temple. Check for birds and countryside background before use. Its baked-in distance makes it less flexible for an onboard bell.
- Neither candidate has been auditioned. No dedicated ship-bell recording was identified in this bundle.

Suggested event sequence for Claude to implement after auditioning:

1. First lone strike as the storm starts, followed by 15–25 seconds of silence.
2. Another quiet strike, then increasingly frequent isolated strikes with 8–14 second gaps.
3. At peak danger, use irregular 4–8 second gaps, varying strike strength slightly and retaining the natural decay. Keep it slower than an alarm.
4. For an onboard bell, position it at a visible bell mounting and animate its sway if a bell mesh exists. For a temple bell, place the sound at the ruin and let its direction guide attention.
5. Experiment with modest downward pitch shifts for weight. Reduce competing wind briefly so the attack reads, and protect crew dialogue. Return to silence after leaving the storm.

Schedule each strike as a separate event with bounded timing variation. A continuous recording or looping toll pattern would prevent deliberate control of the supernatural onset. A single isolated sample may be too repetitive; find two or three clean strikes of the same bell if available.

## Unreal handoff

Use stereo beds for sea and weather. For point effects such as fire, repairs and creature calls, prefer mono sources or make a deliberate mono edit, then use attenuation at the source actor. Standard stereo imports should not receive four-channel quad or Ambix files without channel conversion or the appropriate soundfield setup.

Crossfade weather with the ride phase. Trigger thunder after lightning. Use brief positional impacts at the collision point. Lower competing ambience during crew speech. Audition loop candidates with short equal-power crossfades and vary event timing to limit repetition. Match loudness before setting the final mix.

## Gaps

The shortlist provides useful ingredients, but it does not establish clean open-ocean storm surf, sustained wooden hull creaks, sustained sail flaps, rope tension, stone rubble, cannon reload variations or a music score. The ship asset already provides cannon effects. Parts 4 and 5 mainly contain modern ambiences, crowds and vehicles; their strongest relevant finds are the gurgle and low metal scrape. A ship-repair factory recording is industrial and should be auditioned for isolated squeaks before reuse.
